Story

Part I
The Beginning
Listen to this part

Before there was a dynasty, there was a name. Tesla. She was named not from mythology nor legend, but from a very human wish — a father who longed for a Tesla car received instead something far greater: a cat. A birthday gift from his family. A creature who would, in time, render any automobile entirely irrelevant. Though it must be said — she was never really his. She chose his daughter. The father she merely tolerates.

Her full name, Lady Tesla Diana, carries the weight of three worlds. Diana — the Roman goddess of the hunt, sovereign of the moon and wild things — a name that suits her completely. Diana, too, as in Princess Diana: grace and a quiet refusal to be ordinary. And Diana Foxington, from Bad Guys — elegant, mysterious, never what you expect. She is not a princess. She is something higher. A Lady. Her nickname, to those she permits: Tessie.

Her original name was meant to be Love. But Tesla, as it turned out, is not particularly full of it. The name did not survive contact with her personality. Lady Tesla Diana did.

She was acquired from a home in Georgia for precisely one hundred Former Federal Reserve gold notes — a sum appropriate for acquiring a creature of such considerable consequence. Six weeks old, believed at the time to be a Manx — but she is something more refined: an American Shorthair, not full-blooded perhaps, but fully and entirely perfect. The first time her person laid eyes on her, she was tucked beneath the passenger seat of a car, a small sovereign already claiming her first territory.

She lived first in Florida, then in Alabama. Born into a household of four children — three of them careless with her, rough in the way that children sometimes are with small, fragile things. She was handled without ceremony. The soft kitten began to harden at the edges. She did not forget.

When the family left for a six-week trip, Tesla was left with her grandmother. She waited. She endured. A Lady does not beg to be understood — she simply exists, and lets others catch up.

Part II
Missing
Listen to this part

Alabama. Tesla was one year old — no longer a kitten, already a presence. And then, without warning, she was gone.

Three days. Three days of names called into humid Southern air. Three days in which the world felt slightly wrong. It was the kind of absence that makes a person understand how much space one small creature can occupy in a life.

During those three days, a puppy arrived as if to fill the silence. Maximus Aurelius — a German Shepherd of enormous enthusiasm and no discretion. He did not know he was joining a queendom. He would learn.

Tesla was found beneath her grandmother's house. When she emerged, she was genuinely glad — visibly happy to be found. For a brief moment, she was the soft cat she might have been, had the world been gentler with her.

Then she met Maximus. The happiness lasted precisely as long as it took her to register his existence. A year passed in uneasy truce. She tolerated. She endured. She ruled from a distance.

Part III
Growing Family
Listen to this part

Tesla was three when the next disruption arrived. A kitten, three weeks old. His name was Kableton — Kable, Babytons, Lord Kableton, depending on the day. Intelligent beyond comfort. Relentlessly present. He had opinions about everything and shared all of them. Tesla tolerated him, distantly, which was the highest honor she offered. She never warmed to him. She never really would.

Then Maximus died. Unexpectedly. One year and ten months old — a German Shepherd who never got to grow into the dog he was becoming. Kableton grieved, then curdled that grief into restlessness. He began to bother Tesla. Endlessly. There were fights.

It was around this time that she became Tesla Diana. The name change was not dramatic. It simply became true.

Three months later: Tobias. A full-blooded German Shepherd — a puppy when he arrived, already enormous, a dog whose joy was uncontainable and whose presence filled every room. Toby was overly playful, endlessly affectionate, and utterly without subtlety. Tesla regarded him with the patience of someone waiting for a phase to pass.

Two months after that, on the day after Thanksgiving in Burlington, Iowa — two more. Cat brothers, about a year old. O'Malley (OJ) and Berlioz (Burlie). They arrived on a holiday and immediately made themselves at home.

It was chaos. Too many for one Lady. But of them all, she found she could bear O'Malley. More than that — O'Malley and Berlioz had formed their own bond, a tight and loyal brotherhood that was plain to anyone watching. Where one was, the other was near. But it was O'Malley alone who somehow earned Tesla's guarded acceptance. Not love. Tesla Diana does not love easily. But his presence did not disturb her. He was quiet where Kableton was loud, still where Kableton was chaos. She allowed him near. In this court, that meant everything.

Kableton remained, as ever, very, very, very, very annoying. He and Tesla never got along. They never would. He was simply too much — too loud, too clever, too insistent on existing in every space she occupied. She found him exhausting. He did not care.

Part IV
The Trip
Listen to this part

December. The decision was made. The cats would travel.

They were loaded into a trailer with air conditioning — a mobile kingdom, checked on five times daily. From Iowa to Colorado. Two weeks. Christmas into the new year.

What happens when four cats with complicated relationships share close quarters for a fortnight? Something unexpected. By the end of the two weeks, they seemed closer. Something had softened. O'Malley and Berlioz, already bonded, grew closer still — their brotherhood deepening into something solid and quiet. Even Tesla Diana, sovereign of her own silence, seemed to acknowledge, just slightly, that these creatures were hers. Even Kableton. Even then.

It was on this trip that the court received its formal titles.

And Toby — the great German Shepherd who had arrived as an enormous, joyful puppy — was there too. He had come to the family in September 2025, a puppy already the size of a small bear, overly playful and endlessly loving. He filled every room. He was adored. But he did not stay. He died unexpectedly in June 2026, not yet a year into his time with them, and left behind a silence that no amount of cat drama could fill. The court grieved in its way. Tesla Diana grieved privately.

Part V
The Jester, The Knight, The Lord & The Lady
Listen to this part

Now there are four. The dog is gone. The house belongs to the cats — which is, of course, precisely as Tesla Diana always intended. She is The Lady. They are all, in their way, beneath her. Even when they don't act like it.

Kableton — The Lord. Intelligent beyond what is comfortable, insufferable beyond what is reasonable. He has never once done anything he was told, and sees no reason to start. He and Tesla Diana have never gotten along, not really, not ever — he is too loud, too present, too relentlessly there. He annoys her. She ignores him. He escalates. She leaves the room. The cycle is eternal and she finds it exhausting. He considers it a game. He is, by nearly every account, very, very, very, very annoying.

Berlioz — The Knight. Steadfast and dignified, he holds his position with quiet solemnity. He and O'Malley share a bond that is one of the genuinely beautiful things about this court — tight, loyal, present. Where O'Malley goes, Berlioz is not far. He bickers with Kableton endlessly, as knights and lords will do, but his loyalty to his brother and to the court is unquestioned. Berlioz is great. This is simply a fact.

O'Malley — The Jester. The fool of the court, and yet — the one Tesla Diana has chosen to tolerate above all others. She does not love him in the way humans love. But she allows him near. She does not immediately leave when he enters a room. For a cat who has spent her life guarding the perimeter of her own heart, this is a profound thing. O'Malley, for his part, is steady and loyal — bonded to Berlioz, gentle enough for Tesla, present without being intrusive. He has earned her peace.

And then there is Tesla Diana. Tessie.

She has been a kitten in Georgia. A stowaway under a car seat. A missing cat found under a grandmother's house. A reluctant queen in Alabama and Iowa. A traveler through a Colorado winter. She has outlasted dogs who loved her too loudly, cats who annoyed her beyond reason, and three careless siblings who never quite understood what they were handling.

She was acquired for one hundred Former Federal Reserve gold notes. She became a goddess in practice. She is an American Shorthair — not full-blooded, perhaps, but fully, entirely, perfectly herself.

She does not ask to be worshipped. She simply is — and expects the rest of creation to organize itself accordingly.

Jokes of the Jester
Spoken by Lord O'Malley, The Jester

Why did O’Malley roll a single kibble across the kitchen tile for three hours? He called it an elite training exercise—until it rolled under the fridge, and he claimed the asset was “successfully archived.”

Why does O’Malley have immunity in the court? He says Kableton is an immovable object and Tesla is an unstoppable force, but since he is a liquid, he just pours himself into a sunny spot and melts.

Why did O’Malley lose a fight to the bathroom hallway? He jumped up and hissed, then got amazed that the “other cat” knew his exact strategy.

Why did O’Malley yell “tell my family I love them” at midnight? He was chasing the red dot when it landed on him, and he thought it was the end. Till Kableton turned it off.

O’Malley backed into a tight cardboard box until it got stuck over his head. He spent the afternoon walking blindly into walls, cheerfully announcing that someone turned off the lights and made the kingdom very bumpy.

The Lord's Great Battle
As told by Lord Kableton, who was there

One ordinary afternoon, a dragon appeared in the window. Its scales shimmered. Its breath steamed. Kableton, who has never once backed away from a challenge he did not fully understand, puffed up to twice his size, announced to the court that the beast would not be permitted to enter, and charged.

Then he reached the edge of the counter, looked down, and reconsidered.

He ran. He ran under the couch, through the laundry basket, and straight into the cabinet of pots, which fell on him with tremendous dignity. Berlioz, who had watched from his perch with the patience of a Knight and the expression of someone who had seen this before, climbed down, looked the dragon in the eye, and sat there until it went away.

Kableton emerged an hour later, covered in dust and a dish towel, and has described the battle at every meal since. In his telling, the dragon has grown to the size of the house, the fight lasted three days, and he was wounded in four places. Berlioz has never corrected him. He has, however, been seen to roll his eyes so hard he nearly fell off the shelf.

The Midnight War
By Berlioz the Knight

It was a dark and foggy night when an army of mice marched into the kitchen with toothpicks, ready for war. When we saw them, Kableton screamed like a puppy and crashed straight into the pantry. O’Malley, who was barely clinging to his last two remaining brain cells, immediately went to sleep in a laundry basket, leaving me all by myself again for the third time this month. Finally, after a couple of mice died in the bloody battle, Lady Tesla said from her bed, “This is an interesting war, but I need to go get my beauty sleep. I’ve been up for 6 minutes.” The lead mouse explained that they only invaded because a cat ate the Three Blind Mice. I looked over at the pantry, and Kableton was crawling out. It was him.

I pointed a dramatic paw at him and yelled, “I declare we should execute Kableton for the triple homicide of the Three Blind Mice, and worst of all, causing capital annoyance of the first degree!” Kableton looked at me and said, “What do you mean capital annoyance of the first degree? And the mice were simply just delicious.” We don’t really care about the mice—they’re forever delicious. So fine, you’re off on the triple homicide, but you woke up the Lady, and that is the worst crime known to all cats. We all looked up to Lady Tesla. She said, “I looked at Kableton a couple of hours ago, and he came clean and gave me the mice. I let him have the spleen, because spleens are disgusting.” So the court decided that Kableton had 10 hours of litter box chores and had to fluff Tesla’s pillow for an hour. Of course, Kable skipped out on the litter box, but Tesla got a fluffed pillow.

American Domestic Cat — 61%

The majority of her blood. Descended from cats that arrived in North America with European settlers, who valued them for keeping ships and homes free of rodents.

European Domestic Cat — 13%

Hardy, practical cats from the old country, shaped by centuries of life alongside people.

Maine Coon — 10%

Maine's official state cat. Known for tufted ears, a long shaggy coat, and a large frame built for harsh winters.

Scottish Fold — 7%

Known for ears that fold forward and down, giving an owl-like, gentle expression, and a sweet, affectionate nature.

British Shorthair — 6%

A stocky, round-faced breed with a dense, plush coat, known for a calm and dignified manner.

Sphynx — 3%

Famously hairless, though not truly bare. A fine peach-fuzz covers the skin. The breed was developed in Toronto, Canada, in 1966.

Temperament

Tesla has very low separation anxiety. When her people walk out the door, she does not wait by it. She simply carries on as the sovereign she is, a composure she likely inherited from her independent ancestors.
